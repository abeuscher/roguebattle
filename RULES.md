# Landwar — Rules, as implemented

This describes how the game actually plays right now, not the full design (that's `SPEC.md`). It's written for a player, not a developer. Almost every number below is a placeholder for balance purposes (per `SPEC.md` §22) and will change; what's described as a *mechanic* is the stable part.

**Keep this current.** Whenever a session adds, changes or finishes a game rule, update the relevant section here (or add one) in the same session. The "Not yet in the game" section at the bottom is the checklist — move an item out of it once it's real, instead of leaving this file stale.

---

## Starting a map

- **Title → Start → Configurator → Game summary → the map.** The configurator sets every map-generation option (size, land shape, terrain amounts, building density, road coverage, neutral density, victory/defeat toggles, squad cap, a fairness toggle), with a seed you can randomize and a live minimap preview. Settings plus seed can be copied as a short share code and reproduce the exact same map. The Game summary has a **Your name** field, filled with a random name you can change; your Opinion Leader takes that name (and their squad is named after it).
- **Map size:** width and height in hexes, each its own slider (400×225 — Small — by default). Landing exactly on Small, Medium (800×450) or Large (1200×675) shows that name next to the numbers; any other combination just shows the numbers. A much smaller custom map generates and plays through in a fraction of the time, handy for a quick playtest.
- **Game mode** (the configurator's first setting): **Single map** (the default) or **Campaign start**. They differ only in how your army starts — see "Your starting army" below. Single map mode also has an **XP rate** (150% by default, 100–300%) that multiplies every unit's battle XP, so units advance further within one map; the campaign start ignores it.
- **Map 1's setup** in the campaign start gives you 6 starting squads, the Opinion Leader on the map, 3 random cards, 3 tech points, starting gold (see "Buildings and the economy") and a starting kit of items and supplies — see "Your starting army" below.
- **Building counts follow the map's land:** the default map gets 16 towns, 10 towers, 4 temples and 2 nomad camps (times the density setting); a map with more land gets proportionally more, one with less gets fewer. Hidden sites and lairs scale the same way. An islands map has less land, so fewer buildings than a continent of the same size.
- The game opens on the battlemap with your one deployed squad standing on the Player Base. In single map mode Edit Units opens over it straight away, so you build your squads first.

## The map

- **Hex grid**, axial coordinates, rendered in chunks for performance on large maps.
- **Terrain and difficulty for a squad not native to it:** Plains 1, Forest/Desert/Snow 2, Mountain/Swamp 3, Shallow water 3, Deep water/Lava/Cliffs/Air gaps impassable to ground squads. A squad's own travel-type terrain always counts as difficulty 1 for it, whatever the listed difficulty is. Every landmass is guaranteed reachable on foot from the rest of the map (a shallow ford, a mountain pass, a bridge, or similar), even if slow.
- **Map presets** (Continent, Islands, Archipelago, Floating Isles, Highlands, River Valley, Deep Forest, Tundra, Volcanic, No Ocean) change the terrain mix and favor different squad types; Continent is the default and most balanced. No Ocean is all land. Land % goes up to 100, except that Islands and Floating Isles stop at 70 and Archipelago at 60 so they keep their sea.
- **Getting to islands on foot:** every island is joined to the mainland by shallows you can wade, shaped like reefs or sandbars, wider than one hex and winding; a sizable island gets up to two more such crossings to its nearest neighbours. Roads never cross open sea, so an island's buildings have their own road network.
- **Buildings:** Player Base and Enemy Base (spawn points), towns and towers (liberate by standing a living ground unit in one for 60 ticks — twice as fast with a Sapper-line unit), temples (pay to heal/revive; free slow healing for anyone; taken like a town, but with no liberation rewards, and the enemy goes after them too, as a low priority), nomad camps (a town that relocates every half day and always has a shop), and hidden sites (see below).
- **Roads** connect the visible buildings and bases; a non-flying squad moves at ×0.75 time on them.
- **Day and night:** day 06:00–18:00, night 18:00–06:00. Holy units fight +15% by day and −15% at night; evil units the reverse; neutral units are unaffected.
- **Game speed:** five tiers (×0.25, ×0.5, ×1, ×2, ×4), PgUp/PgDn to change, Space to pause. A day at normal speed takes 5 real minutes. Opening any menu or a battle pauses the clock too. A new map starts paused, with "Press Play to begin" in the message banner until you first start time.
- **The screen:** gold (a coin, the amount and the day's income and upkeep) and the reputation meter (a bar between a sad and a happy face; hover for the number) sit at the top right. Your squads on the map are in a drawer at the middle left: shut, each row shows the squad's leader, number and status; the arrow slides it open to show each squad's travel type, speed and alignment and its name too; click a row to find that squad, or double-click it for the squad menu. **Travel type, speed and alignment** (a small hex of the terrain the squad travels best on, its speed tier, and a sun, half circle or horned head; hover for details) show wherever a squad is shown: the squad menu's header, the drawer, and Edit Units. **Status icons** (in the drawer and the squad menu's header; hover for details): footprints, moving; a house, standing in any building; crossed-out footprints, standing in the open. **Squads on the map** are their leader's icon in a ring with the number on a small tag, no pole or flag; a squad that lost its leader and is crawling home shows a skull instead (on the map and in the drawer). Zoomed out, a squad's click area grows with its token, a little wider still, so squads are easy to grab; where two then overlap, a small menu asks which you meant. **Towns with a shop** have a white dollar sign on the roof. **Flags mean destinations:** click a moving squad and its numbered flag stands where it's headed (the squad menu's **View destination** centres the map there and shows the flag for a few seconds); while you lay a move, the flag is your pointer. **Place names in banners are links:** click one ("The enemy has captured Ashford.") and the map centres on it. The minimap is at the bottom right with the date, time and game speed under it, and the toolbar (Edit Units, Deploy Squad, Cards, Tech) is at the bottom centre. The round **info button** at the bottom left shows or hides the map details and keyboard controls (hidden to start).
- **Fog of war:** terrain is always visible; enemy and neutral squads only show within your nearest squad's sight radius (base 7 hexes, further out while flying or on your own travel terrain, closer in mountains or forest, clamped 5–10 and never shown as a number). **Buildings you hold see too:** a tower 16 hexes around it, a temple 12, a town or the Player Base 8. Hidden sites reveal the same way. Enemy and neutral routes are never drawn on the map, only your own.
- **The log (L)** records every battle action, every map order and arrival, every economic flow, and every progression change, filterable by squad and category. A debug toggle in it shows the hidden numbers behind each entry (hit chance, the actual roll, growth, luck, and so on).

## Squads and movement

- Each squad has a **front row** and a **back row**; a unit's class decides whether it fights (and is attacked) from the front or back. Edit Units puts a unit in the back row when it heals or supports from there, or when its ranged or magic attack there reaches at least as many units as its front attack; otherwise in front. Units reached = hits × units per hit: a plain attack reaches 1 a hit, a row-wide attack 3, a chain or cleave its number (chain 3 = 3), a pierce 2, a hit on everyone 5. So a Sapper (3 melee hits in front, 1 shot behind: 3 against 1) goes in front, and a Siege Engineer (3 melee hits, 1 row-wide shot: 3 against 3, a tie goes back) goes in back. You can still put anyone in either row; this only picks the default and the attack its card shows. Front-row units take 20% less damage from every attack, since they take the brunt.
- **Every squad the game generates starts with its rows evened out**: yours, the enemy's and the neutrals'. 5 units split 3:2 or 2:3, 4 units 2:2, never 4:1. The units moved are the ones that fight about as well from either row. You can still rearrange your own however you like in Edit Units, even while a squad is out in the field. Adding units to a squad or taking them out only works at the Player Base.
- **Slots:** every squad starts with 5 (6 in single map mode). A Small unit costs 1 slot, Large costs 2, and two Tiny units can share a single slot. A leader earns +1 slot at level 5/10/15/20, but only if their charisma is at least 5 × their level at that moment (a Herald or Standard-Bearer in the squad helps toward this).
- **Travel type:** a squad's one native terrain, decided by a weighted vote of its living units' home terrain (Small 1, the leader and Large units 2, Tiny ½; ties go to the leader). Moving through that terrain costs the squad its base speed; other terrain costs more (see difficulty above). A squad that's entirely flyers moves as Sky (flies over everything but air gaps); a squad that's entirely water natives or amphibians can cross deep water.
- **Speed tier** is set by the squad's slowest living unit and decides how fast it crosses a hex; losing units to death slows the squad further, proportional to the slots lost.
- **A squad always has a leader** (in both modes): in Edit Units, only a unit that can lead can start a squad in an empty cell, and the last unit that can lead can't leave a squad that still has others in it (move the others out first, or bring in another leader). A message says why when a drop is refused.
- **Edit Units:** opening a squad (from the map's squad menu, the encounter dialog, or a squad card in Edit Units) always shows the same Edit Squad view. Its title reads "Edit Units > Edit Squad: Kenley (Sapper)"; click "Edit Units" for the whole army. The X at the top right (or Esc, or a click outside) goes back to wherever you opened it from: the army view, or the map. The army view closes with Done (U). Each squad's card shows where it is at the top right and Deploy (or Return) at the bottom right. In a unit's details, the arrows at the screen's edges (or the Left and Right keys) step through its squad: front row left to right, then the back row.
- **The Recruits list** in Edit Units: a gray star marks every recruit who can lead (a gold star is a squad's actual leader). A Sort by menu orders the list by Leader (the default: leaders first, then by stage and level), Class, Level or Name; the choice is kept until you quit. Each recruit is colored by the attack it would use in the row it would be placed in (a healer shows gold, an archer green), with a color key above the list: Melee, Ranged, Magic, Heal / buff, Debuff, Ward, Breath.
- **Deployment:** at the start, only the Opinion Leader's squad is on the map. Other squads deploy from the Player Base (via Edit Units or the Deploy Squad button, which is greyed out when the squad cap is reached or no squad is waiting at base), costing one day of their upkeep in gold, and appear on the Player Base; you lay their route as you deploy them. A squad cap (default 6, set in the configurator) limits how many of your squads can be on the map at once. A squad returns to base by marching there and going in on arrival, or instantly if it's already standing on the Player Base.
- **Enemy squads:** clicking one you haven't fought only says how strong it looks. Once you've fought it, clicking it shows its units (class, level, row, and who had fallen) as they were when that fight ended.
- **Arrivals:** when one of your squads stops at the end of its route, a banner and the log say where ("… has stopped at Ashford").
- **Giving orders:** click a squad to open its menu (this pauses the game); Move lets you click hexes to lay a chain of rally points, and clicking the last point again commits the route. You can plan routes for as many squads as you like, one at a time, while paused.
- **Following:** while laying a move, click a visible enemy or neutral squad that is on the move and choose "Follow" ("Move here" just lays a rally point there; Esc closes the choice). A squad standing still can't be followed: clicking it just lays a rally point there. Clicking that rally point, or the squad standing on it, a second time commits the move without asking again. Your squad heads for it and re-routes whenever it moves, and catching it starts a fight as usual. If it slips out of sight, your squad goes to where it was last seen and stops there. Any new order ends following, and the squad menu shows "Following …" meanwhile.
- **The squad menu** shows the squad and one row of icon buttons: Edit squad (pencil), Use item (flask), Shop or Temple services (a hand with coins), Move (footprints), Portal (in a temple; see "Temples you hold"), View destination (flag, while moving), Ranged attack (crosshair) and Return to base (house). Hover a button for its name and what it does, or why it's greyed out.
- **Closing windows:** an X at the top right closes a window (Esc too). Clicking outside a window also closes it (the squad menu, building, deploy, Use item, shop, tech, log, Edit Units, Cards, options, debug panel, a liberation message, and the Esc menu, which resumes; a yes/no question counts it as no). Windows that need an answer don't: a card choice, recruiting, the encounter dialog and the battle.
- **Squads of yours can share a hex** freely. A hex held by a hostile squad blocks movement; your squad waits at its edge instead of stacking on it.

## Units

- **Stats:** STR, CON (HP = CON × 4), INT, SPD (turn order), AGI (hit and dodge), PDEF, MDEF, plus Alignment (0–100: evil ≤40, neutral 41–59, holy ≥60) and Charisma (0–100).
- **Rolling a new unit:** starts from its class's base stats, adds a random number of "flex" points weighted toward the class's key stats, rolls its alignment inside its class's lean, and gets a unique first and last name (no repeats within a run).
- **Hidden traits**, rolled once and never shown directly: a growth profile (each non-key stat has its own chance to grow at a level-up, guaranteed at least once every 3 levels), a talent tier (Ordinary/Gifted/Prodigy, each raising XP and growth chances), and luck (a little extra crit chance, dodge, and better draws). These only ever show up as a visible effect — a Prodigy's level-up gets a flourish, a lucky unit does well more often.
- **Morale**, per squad (0–100, starts at 50, never shown as a number): falls when the squad's outnumbered in a battle, its leader gets hit, a unit of it dies, it fights off its leader's home terrain, or at the wrong time of day for its alignment (holy by night, evil by day); rises on a win, a liberation, or resting in a temple. Applies to every squad, not just yours — an enemy or neutral one has to be driveable-off too. Below 30, the squad deals 10% less damage; below 25, your leader can try to drive a beaten enemy off the map for good instead of letting it recover, for a charisma-based chance. A log line marks every change.
- **Loyalty**, recruited neutrals only (§16; a unit you roll fresh for a Serf-line squad never has it): rises after a win or while reputation is high, falls sitting unused in the recruit pool. If it drops very low, the unit gets a day's warning (logged) before it might actually leave the army for good on the next day change — never mid-battle.
- **Bonds:** two units that fight 6 battles together in the same squad gain +5% damage and +5% dodge while both stay alive, announced once in the log.
- **XP and levels:** every unit of yours still alive when a battle ends earns XP, whether you won or lost (a quarter as much if you retreated), more against a stronger enemy and with a more charismatic leader. A unit below its squad's average level earns 25% more per level under (at most double), so newcomers catch up. A level costs the class's XP rate × a fixed amount. Key stats always go up a level; other stats follow the hidden growth roll.
- **Promotion** becomes available at level 3, 6 and 9 (into the class's next line stage), only at the Player Base, only if the squad still fits its slots afterward; where a line branches by alignment, the unit's current band decides which branches are open.
- **Leaders:** whichever living unit that can lead was added to the squad first. If the leader is removed, the best remaining leader-capable unit (alive, highest level, highest stage) takes over automatically.

## Battle

- **Trigger:** a player squad and a hostile squad land on the same or adjacent hexes, or you order a ranged attack from the squad menu. The map pauses and the camera glides to the fight, zooming in until the view spans about half the map (if you're already closer, it just pans), then a "X's party has encountered the enemy" dialog appears below the two squads. Use item(s) there lets you use Herbs and Revival Draughts on that squad before the fight (a revived unit fights); Edit Squad opens the squad in Edit Units to change its formation first, and the fight uses the new rows; Begin Battle opens the battle view, which springs open from the fight's place on the map. The battle is played on the defending hex's terrain. The battle view shows both squads and waits for you to press Fight (Space), unless you turn that off in Options. A ranged attack you ordered yourself, or a fight with neutrals (the recruit dialog already announced it), skips the dialog, since you already know it's coming.
- **Turn order:** every living unit with attacks left acts once per round, fastest (SPD) first, until both sides are out of attacks or one side is wiped. A squad that resisted recruitment, or has First strike/Charge, can act first.
- **Targeting:** plain melee hits the front row first (the back row only once the front is empty); ranged and magic can hit any row; several classes have group attacks (hit a whole row, the whole squad, a cleave, a pierce, a chain) per their class sheet.
- **Your controls mid-battle:** switch tactic any time (Best/Strong/Weak/Leader — who your units focus), retreat, or play any cards in your hand. **Each squad keeps the tactic you last picked for it** into its next battles (and through a save) until you change it; a new squad starts on Best. Cards opens your hand large in the middle of the screen and the battle waits while it's open; Play takes effect at once, before the next attack (even before the first one, while the battle waits for Fight, and before a round's first attack while its banner shows); an attack already under way finishes first. The X (or Esc) goes back.
- **The battle view:** both squads on a field of the battle's terrain, each squad's name on a small tag above it, and in the middle a "vs." box with the terrain, time of day and who attacked. Your squad is always on the left, whoever started the fight. One button at the field's bottom left reads Fight before the battle starts, then becomes a small round Pause or Play icon (Space works too). While the battle plays, only the field shows, in an even frame with the space below it see-through, and the latest action is written beside the button; pause it and the play-by-play log and the other controls (speed, tactic, retreat, cards, items, skip) show under the field. The log marks each round "Begin Round N". A speed you pick in a battle is kept for the next battles. **What you see of the enemy:** only its HP bars, with no HP numbers and no attack labels ("Melee ×2"), and the log says how hard each blow landed but not what's left. A living Scout or Hussar (the Scout line) in your squad shows the enemy's numbers and attacks in full.
- **The result:** when the battle ends, the field goes away and a summary takes its place. At the top it says Won, Lost, Retreated or Draw, from your side. Under that are two columns, your squad on the left and the enemy on the right, each with HP lost and how many units fell (no names). Then XP and level-ups, the offer to drive a shaken enemy off, and Continue. After a fight with neutrals the map's message reads "<your squad> wins" or "loses", plus "The rats are beaten back and return to the plains" or "The rats are victorious and return to the plains", with the battle's terrain.
- **Damage:** physical is (STR + weapon) × modifiers − (PDEF + armor); magic is (INT + spell power) × modifiers − MDEF, then an element multiplier; healing scales with INT. Hit chance is 85% plus the attacker's AGI edge over the target's, minus a little per target luck, clamped 50–95% (never shown as the raw number — you see the shaped, fairer version: two dice averaged, a guaranteed hit after 3 misses in a row, and crit chance that climbs after every non-crit). Damage always varies ±10%.
- **Modifiers** that multiply together: home terrain (+15%, or −15% on difficulty-3 terrain away from home), day/night by alignment, element match-ups (strong/weak pairs), a few class-vs-class bonuses (archers vs flyers, mounted vs mounted, and so on), the defending building's bonus, and each class's own traits and auras from the sheet.
- **Status effects** (poison, burn, freeze, shock, petrify, sleep, charm, blind, silence, fear, weak, haste, protect, regen, cleanse) all clear at the end of the battle; a unit native to an element's terrain resists the matching one.
- **Retreat:** your chance is 50% plus 20% per speed tier you have on the enemy, clamped 10–90%. Success steps your squad back a hex and costs each unit a point of charisma; failure costs the squad its next action.
- **Outcomes:** a wiped-out squad is carried back to its base needing full revival; a squad that loses its leader marches home on its own at half speed (reviving the leader in time cancels this); otherwise the side that lost the larger HP share gets pushed back 1 hex (2 if it wasn't on its travel terrain). A squad keeps its current orders after a battle unless it was pushed back, retreated, wiped out, or lost its leader. If the beaten squad still stands on the winner's route, the winner goes round it and carries on (for a short while after a fight the two won't fight again). An enemy squad that beats one of yours stands still for 2 game hours afterwards, so you can come back at it. If a beaten enemy's morale is low enough, your leader can try to drive it off the map for good instead of letting it recover (see "Morale" above); on success your squad earns XP as if it had beaten them: each living unit gets the battle's XP again. The battle results say when this is on offer ("Demand they flee", with your leader's charisma as the chance, halved if their morale is 10–25), and after a win while it isn't yet, that it will be once their spirit breaks. It's offered only at a 25% chance or better; below that the results say your leader lacks the charisma. Driving off the Warlord wins the game just as wiping out his squad does. After a fight on the Enemy Base, the results also remind you that the enemy's fallen come back only slowly there.

## Buildings and the economy

- **Towns have names** (Ashford, Old Rookmere), the same for the same map seed. They show in the building's popup, the liberation message and the log. **Towers have magical names** ("The Tower of Ee", "Amaraz's Tower of Surprises", "The Crooked Spire"), no two alike on a map. **Every temple belongs to one of the twelve Gods of Perinea** and is named for it ("the Temple of Ithrael"; two temples on a map can share a god). Its window shows the god's domain, what they look like and how they show themselves to mortals. Other buildings go by their type.
- **Hidden sites** stay off the map until one of your squads' sight reaches them; then they show what they are. Seeing one gives nothing: a squad has to step onto it.
  - **Treasure** (a chest): 100–250 gold, and half the time an item that needs no tech.
  - **Card cache** (a card): a card (a Seer or Oracle leader lets you choose, as usual).
  - **Rare recruit** (a tent with a banner): a stage 1 unit from a race line (never a sea creature), at the median level of your squads, would join you: a window shows who it is, with Recruit (it joins the recruit pool) or Leave (it stays there, the same unit, for later; step off and back on to be asked again). It counts as a recruit for loyalty.
  - These three are used up: the site leaves the map once claimed.
  - **Secret temple** (a small mossy temple): a temple in every way once found (free slow healing, Temple services). It never shows on the minimap.
  - **Lair** (a cave): a neutral band native to the ground there guards it and never leaves it, as strong as a roaming band at that distance would be. Meeting it offers Recruit or Fight as with any neutral band, and after that it's gone either way. From then on any squad standing there defends +10% and heals 10% a day.
  - A town with local news points you at the nearest unseen site of any kind.
- **Clicking a building** anywhere on its picture opens its window. A building you don't hold that none of your squads can see shows only its name, who holds it (no one or the enemy) and whether it has a shop; the rest (defense, liberation progress, local news) shows once a squad is in sight or you hold it.
- **Liberation message:** names the place and shows any card drawn on the left, with View all cards under it (it shows your hand as cards, over the message); any conscripts who joined (single map mode) are listed on the right with their icon, name, class and level. The X closes it. When it closes, a small fireworks show bursts over the place, so you can tell which it was in a cluster. "Do not show this window again" (or Options) turns the message off: a banner names the place instead and the fireworks still play. It always opens when there's a card to choose.
- **Enemy captures:** a banner says when the enemy captures a neutral town or tower ("The enemy has captured Ashford."). When it retakes one of yours, a window says so instead ("Ashford is lost"), the map centres on the place, and it costs 3 reputation.
- **Every building starts neutral** except the two bases: no one holds a town or tower at the start, so both sides race out to take them. You can shop in, rest in and use any building the enemy doesn't hold; you only earn income from the ones you hold.
- **Liberating** a town or tower, neutral or enemy-held (stand a living ground unit there for 60 ticks, twice as fast with a Sapper) flips its owner, gives reputation and alignment swings, +5 charisma to a matching-alignment leader, a gold bonus, a card, and a tech point. A popup names what you took, focuses the camera on it, and shows the card you drew (or lets you pick one, if a Seer or Oracle offers a choice) right there.
- **Temples you hold:** taking a temple gives no bonus gold, reputation or card, and losing one costs no reputation (a "lost" window still shows). Taking one opens a window like a liberation's ("Temple of Hasketh taken!"), with its god and what holding it gives; the liberation option turns it off the same way. A held temple sees 12 hexes around it. **Portal:** a squad standing still in a temple you hold gets a Portal button (an orbit) in its squad menu. Pressing it returns you to the map, which pulls back to show the squad and every other temple you hold; those temples glow with pulsing rings. Click one and a question asks you to confirm ("Step through" or Cancel, which goes back to picking); then the squad is there at once, with the map following it. Esc goes back to the squad menu. In a temple you don't hold the button is greyed out and says why. **Training:** at each day's start, a squad standing in a temple you hold earns XP for each living unit: 30 ÷ its level (at least 1, times the XP rate), so a level 1 unit gets 30 and a level 6 one 5.
- **Resting in a building** heals a squad's living units a share of their max HP each day: 15% in your own base, 15% in a town/tower matching your alignment, 10% otherwise, 5% in a temple. Squads and the recruit pool at the Player Base heal the base's 15% too.
- **Income and upkeep**, settled every noon from day 2 (day 1 runs without one): you earn 50 gold per town/tower you hold, then each squad on the map pays upkeep for its units (fallen ones included). The recruit pool and squads at base cost nothing. A unit's upkeep goes by its stage and size (5/10/20/35 by stage, Large ×2), times the map's towns and towers ÷ 28 (a Small map's count), so a bigger map costs more and a tiny one less, in step with what it can pay. The HUD shows the day's income and upkeep next to your gold.
- **Starting gold** is 2 days' upkeep of a full field army on that map (the squad cap of 6-unit stage 2 squads), at least 500: 720 on a Small map.
- **Debt:** upkeep is paid even when the gold runs out, so gold can go below zero at noon. While in debt nothing can be bought (shops, Serfs, temple services, deploying); the gold shows red with "In debt 1/3", and a banner and the noon ledger say so. **Three noons running in debt, or debt past 2 days' upkeep of a full field army** (720 on a Small map), is a defeat. Getting back above zero resets the count. At noon the game pauses and shows the day's ledger: every gold change since the last noon (liberation bonuses, purchases, deploys, temple services, sales, income, each squad's upkeep), the net for the day and the gold now. "Do not show this window again" (or Options) turns it off; a one-line summary shows in the banner instead.
- **Items** belong to the whole army: from Edit Units, any free item can go on any unit that can use it, and be taken off or swapped, wherever that unit's squad is, at base or out in the field.
- **Going broke:** at zero gold or in debt, any squad can be sold from Edit Units for a gold value based on its units' stages; it leaves the army, the map and loses its items.
- **Shops** open from the squad menu when a squad stands still in a building with one (not enemy-held). Towns and nomad camps also sell Serfs into your recruit pool. Every shop stocks Herbs, Revival Draughts, 4 of the 8 cure potions (which 4 is fixed per shop) and 1–6 kinds of random item, 1–4 of each (fixed per shop), priced by your reputation (and cheaper still for a squad with a Smuggler or Fence). Stock is limited: each line shows how many are left, and every shop refills to full at noon. Temples sell healing and revival from the same screen. **One shop on each map holds a very rare item** with a big buff (Sunblade, Stormbow, Dragonscale or the Crown of Ages, 400–450 gold); once bought it isn't restocked.
- **Services and consumables:** heal a hurt unit to full, or revive a fallen one, for gold — only at the Player Base, in the recruit pool, or for a squad standing still in a temple. Carried consumables:
  - **Herb** (+30 HP) and **Revival Draught** (a fallen unit back at full HP; reviving a leader cancels its march home): use them anywhere on the map from the squad menu's Use item.
  - **Cure potions**, one per status: Antidote (poison), Burn Salve, Thaw Tonic (freeze), Insulating Balm (shock), Softening Oil (petrify), Smelling Salts (sleep), Eye Drops (blind) and Echo Herb (silence). Use them in battle from the Items button, which lists only potions that fit a status one of your units has. A potion takes effect at once, like a card, and doesn't cost a turn. Statuses all clear after a battle anyway.

## Reputation, charisma and alignment

- **Reputation** is one army-wide 0–100 meter (starting at 50). It rises or falls with liberations and with time spent in a matching-alignment building, falls by 3 when the enemy retakes a building of yours, moving opposite ways for holy vs. evil squads (neutral squads don't move it). It sets your liberation bonus size, shop prices (cheaper near 100, pricier near 0) and your chance to recruit a neutral squad you meet — doubled for a squad with a Smuggler or Fence on prices.
- **Charisma**, per unit: up from liberating a matching building (leader only), from beating a notably stronger enemy; down from retreating or beating a much weaker one.
- **Alignment**, per unit, each battle: up against an even-or-stronger enemy, down against a weaker one; liberating a town pushes every unit's alignment toward holy, a tower toward evil.

Cards always show as their pictures: in the Cards window, the battle's card picker, a liberation's draw or choice, and when one is played in battle (the card itself appears mid-field for a moment). No limit on cards held or played. Every card is equally likely except Phoenix, which is rare (about 1 draw in 100).

## Tech tree

- One army-wide tree, four tiers, six branches (Arms, Archery, Arcana, Cavalry, Beasts and flyers, Logistics) plus a terrain node per terrain per tier. You start with 3 tech points, earning more from liberations, battle wins, and a bonus for finishing the map in victory.
- A node costs its tier number in points. A tier opens once 80% of the previous tier's branch nodes (not counting terrain nodes) are bought. Spend any time from the Tech screen.

## Your starting army (Map 1 setup)

**Single map mode:**
- **60 units, and you build the squads** (doubled on 2026-11-11 for testing: everything below is twice the base pool, one setting in `rules.json`). The Opinion Leader (a stage-3 leader) starts alone in squad 1, on the map at the Player Base. The other 59 wait in the recruit pool: more stage-3 leaders (twice one more than the "Squads on the map" cap, counting the Opinion Leader: 14 on Small, 18 on Medium, 22 on Large), 6 stage-1 Large units, 20 Serfs, and stage-2 units for the rest (20 on Small, 16 on Medium, 12 on Large). All from the Serf lines except the Large units, which come from the race lines (Drakeling, Wolf, Bison and the like; never a sea creature) — weak for their 2 slots at first, strong once promoted.
- Between them the leaders cover all 5 Serf-line terrains (Plains, Forest, Swamp, Mountain, Desert) before any repeats. The stage-2 units include at least 8 front-line units and 8 healers or support units (6 and 6 on a Large map, where there are only 12).
- **Squads hold 6 slots** (instead of 5), growing from there as usual.
- **Leaders decide how many squads you field.** Every squad needs a unit that can lead (stage 3 or 4; see "A squad always has a leader" above). You start with leaders to spare for the map cap (twice what's needed, while the pool is doubled); more come as units promote to stage 3 or leaders join, up to the 16 squads the army holds.
- **The Opinion Leader can move to any squad** or wait in the pool. Only their death loses the map (when that defeat is on), not squad 1 breaking up.
- **Squads on the map** default to the map's size: 6 on Small, 8 on Medium, 10 on Large (the nearest size for a custom map). You can still change it in the configurator.
- **Conscription:** the first time you liberate a town (or nomad camp; not a tower), 1–3 fighters (Serf-line stage 2, at the median level of your squads) and a Serf join the recruit pool at the Player Base. If no one in the pool can lead, one of the fighters is a leader. Each town pays you once, however often it changes hands. The liberation message lists who joined.
- **The enemy grows too, at half your rate:** the first time it takes a town, 1–2 fighters (no Serf) join a pool at the Enemy Base, at its squads' median level. Once that pool holds 5 with a leader among them, they form a new Warband that marches out. The enemy never fields more than 1.5× the squads it started with.
- **Enemy squads are bigger:** each has 5–6 units (its usual 4 plus 1–2 stage 2 units) and 6 slots, to match your squads of 6.
- Cards, tech points, gold, items and supplies as below.

**Campaign start:**
- **6 squads**, each a stage-3 leader plus four stage-2 units, rolled only from the basic "Serf" promotion lines (never the rarer race lines).
- Between the 6 leaders, every terrain a Serf line can reach (Plains, Forest, Swamp, Mountain, Desert) is covered by at least one squad's leader. Each squad also always has at least one front-line unit and one healer-or-support unit; everything else about the roll is random.
- One of the 6, chosen at random, is the Opinion Leader's squad — the only one that starts deployed, standing on the Player Base. The other 5 wait in your Edit Units roster.
- You also start with 3 random cards, 3 tech points, 500 gold, 6 random items that need no tech (in the Items tab of Edit Units), 5 Revival Draughts and 20 Herbs.
- The squad cap defaults to 6, so all six squads can be out at once.

## The enemy

The enemy fields half as many squads as the map has towns (at least 3; 8 on the default map). Each has 4 units, 2 front and 2 back, and no healers (5–6 units in single map mode, which also adds conscripted Warbands; see "Your starting army"). Every one starts on the Enemy Base, just as yours start at your base, except that **the enemy starts holding 1 or 2 towns** (at random), with one of its field squads already on each. These towns are picked at random, more likely the nearer they are to the point midway between the two bases, so fighting starts sooner and less all at once. They aren't extra squads, just field squads that start forward:

- **The Warlord and a Base guard** stay on the Enemy Base and never go looking for a fight. They only move if pushed back from losing a fight there, or to come back after one.
- **Field squads** (the rest) march out to take towns and towers. Standing on one the enemy doesn't hold, a field squad stays until it's taken. Then it weighs holding it against moving on to another neutral or player-held town or tower that no other enemy squad is heading for:
  - Towns are worth more than towers, buildings toward the middle of the map are worth more than ones near either base, and a building you hold is worth double.
  - Holding is worth more the more of your strength is close by. It's worth little when nothing threatens the building, and nothing if another enemy squad already holds it.
  - A target is worth less the farther away it is.
  - It takes the better choice about 80% of the time and decides at random the rest. A squad that holds thinks again a few hours later.

**Enemy units only come back at the Enemy Base, and slowly.** Any enemy squad that has lost half or more of its units drops what it's doing and heads home (if a fight on the way pushes it back, it sets off home again). On the base, its fallen come back one at a time, leader first, at full HP, one every 2 game hours; it stays until all are back, then returns to its post (or its next target). Living units don't heal on the Enemy Base at all. An enemy squad with lighter losses carries its fallen until it next stands on the base. So the Warlord can be worn down: hit him with several squads in quick succession before his fallen come back. The enemy can retake a building the same way you liberate one: a living ground unit of theirs sitting unopposed in it for 60 ticks flips it back, including your own Player Base.

## Neutrals and recruiting

- **How many:** the Neutrals Density setting decides how many lairs there are (about 11, 14 or 20 on the default map for low, medium and high), one roaming squad each.
- **Lairs** are scattered off-road, each home to one race (never a Serf-line class) native to that spot's terrain; the further a lair is from the Player Base, the stronger and higher-level its squad. Unlike towns and towers, you can't tell a lair is there until a squad's sight reaches it.
- Each lair's squad **roams** a short patrol around its lair, chasing down the nearest player or enemy squad that wanders into range; with nothing nearby it just wanders near home. Neutrals fight both you and the enemy — an enemy squad that stumbles into one fights it out on its own, with no pause for you to watch.
- **Meeting one yourself** glides the camera to it, like a battle, and offers a choice: you're told its class, not how many of them there are, then pick Recruit or Fight.
  - **Recruit** rolls a chance from the neutrals' numbers, how far your squad's alignment is from theirs, and your reputation; success adds them to your recruit pool for free and gives your leader +3 charisma, no fight at all. Failure means they fight you having resisted — they get a full round of attacks before you do. Either way the same dialog then tells you what happened before anything else: who joined, or that they refused, with the chance and the main thing that held it down.
  - **Fight** skips the roll and goes straight into a normal battle.
- **A second chance:** if you win the battle without wiping them out, the dialog asks once more: Recruit (the same chance, worked out on who's left) or Let them go. They join at the HP they have left; if they refuse, or you let them go, they go home.
- **You meet each neutral squad once.** After you recruit it or fight it, win or lose, it leaves the map; it never comes back at you as a second attacking force.
- This is the only way the rarer race lines (the sheet's R, N, L and T lines) enter your army — they never appear in a starting squad or an ordinary promotion.

## Victory and defeat

- The configurator turns each condition on or off; any one you've enabled ends the map the moment it's met (checked every tick). If both a defeat and a victory condition are met on the same tick, defeat wins.
- **Taking the Enemy Base** (liberating it, as with any building) wins at once, whatever conditions you've set.
- **Victory**, any you've enabled: defeat the enemy leader (on by default — their squad never moves from the Enemy Base, so this always means beating or driving it off there); reach the goal (stand a living squad on the Enemy Base's hex); defeat a set number of enemy squads, or all of them; hold a share of the map's towns and towers for a set stretch of time running (losing the share partway through resets the clock); or just survive until a timer runs out.
- **Defeat**, any you've enabled: the Opinion Leader dies (on by default); the Player Base is captured — the enemy takes it the same way you liberate a town, so this only becomes a real threat once you're not holding ground for them to march on instead. Always on: **death by penury**, three noons running in debt or debt past the limit (see "Debt").
- The map freezes the instant it ends — no more movement, battles or ticking — and a summary shows the result, the day you ended on, your gold and reputation, enemy squads defeated, and towns held. From there the only way out is back to the title; there's no second map yet.

## Saving and loading

- **Save game** in the Esc menu opens 3 slots, each showing its day and time, gold, squads on the map and when it was saved (or "Empty"). Saving over a slot in use asks first. There's no autosave.
- Not during a battle or an encounter (the button is greyed out, and says why), and not once the map is over.
- **Return to title** and **Quit** ask "Save your game before leaving?": Save first (pick a slot, then leave), Leave without saving, or Cancel.
- **The start screen** shows **Continue** (loads the most recent save) and **Load game** (pick a slot) once any slot is in use. A loaded game opens paused, exactly as it was saved, and plays on just as it would have.
- A save from an older version of the game, or a damaged file, is refused with a message rather than loaded.

## Options

While time is stopped on the map, a large pause sign and "Paused" show at the top left.

Options on the Start screen and in the in-game menu (Esc) hold your own settings, saved between sessions. **Sound** comes first: a master volume (80 to start), Music (70), Effects (80) and Voice (100) sliders, and Mute all sound; changes are heard at once. The switches below are all on by default: whether a battle shows both squads and waits for Fight (a checkbox next to Fight turns it off too), whether switching to another window pauses the game (the battle, if one is playing, otherwise the map; you resume it yourself), the noon ledger, the liberation message, and player tips. The game also remembers the battle speed you last picked, and which tips you've seen.

## Testing tools (debug panel)

Backtick opens the debug panel. It's for testers only and isn't part of the game. Each tab groups its tools in labelled sections; close it with the X, backtick or Esc. "At the cursor" means the hex under the mouse when you opened the panel.

- **Map:** reveal every enemy and hidden site; a fresh map from any seed with the same settings; map overlays (terrain, movement cost, roads, buildings per quadrant, lair danger), the hex grid, and your squads' sight radius.
- **Time:** jump to any hour, run the map on for N hours, or freeze the time of day while everything else keeps going.
- **Units:** *Build a squad*: an empty squad is shown in front and back rows; pick a class and a level and Add puts a freshly rolled unit of it into the squad (any mix of classes, up to a full squad, each in its usual row; the first that can lead leads). Click a unit to select it, then Remove. Pick a side and Place at cursor puts a copy on the map; the built squad stays in the panel, even after closing, to place again, until Clear. *A squad on the map*: pick one, then set its alignment, teleport it to the cursor or remove it. *Your army*: heal and revive all your units; the Wide roster button (16 varied squads).
- **Resources:** add gold, tech points or any card.
- **Battle:** force every battle to a win, loss or draw; replay the last battle with the same rolls (on copies, so nothing on the map changes; cards played and tactic changes made the first time aren't replayed); show hidden numbers in the log and battle view; switch off each fairness rule.
- **AI:** show every enemy and neutral squad's role and whether it's moving, with a line to its target; freeze enemy and neutral AI.
- **Simulation:** fight two squads' builds against each other N times and report win rates and HP lost.

Outside the panel: O cycles overlays, G the grid, H hidden sites, F8 a squad's travel type.
