# Landwar — Rules, as implemented

This describes how the game actually plays right now, not the full design (that's `SPEC.md`). It's written for a player, not a developer. Almost every number below is a placeholder for balance purposes (per `SPEC.md` §22) and will change; what's described as a *mechanic* is the stable part.

**Keep this current.** Whenever a session adds, changes or finishes a game rule, update the relevant section here (or add one) in the same session. The "Not yet in the game" section at the bottom is the checklist — move an item out of it once it's real, instead of leaving this file stale.

---

## Starting a map

- **Quick Start** (on the Start screen, under Start Game) skips the configurator and the Game summary and drops you straight into a small game with your squads already built: see "Quick start" under "Your starting army". A new seed every time.
- **Title → Start → Configurator → Game summary → the map.** The configurator sets every map-generation option (size, land shape, terrain amounts, building density, road coverage, neutral density, victory/defeat toggles, squad cap, a fairness toggle), with a seed you can randomize and a live minimap preview. Settings plus seed can be copied as a short share code and reproduce the exact same map. The Game summary has a **Your name** field, filled with a random name you can change; your Opinion Leader takes that name (and their squad is named after it).
- **Map size:** width and height in hexes, each its own slider (400×225 — Small — by default; as small as 100×45). Landing exactly on Small, Medium (800×450) or Large (1200×675) shows that name next to the numbers; any other combination just shows the numbers. A much smaller custom map generates and plays through in a fraction of the time, handy for a quick playtest.
- **Game mode** (the configurator's first setting): **Single map** (the default), **Campaign start** or **Quick start**. They differ in how your army starts — see "Your starting army" below; Quick start also gives a small map at least 3 towns, 2 towers and a temple, one of the towns 8–15 hexes from your base. Single map mode (and Quick start) also has an **XP rate** (150% by default, 100–300%) that multiplies every unit's battle XP, so units advance further within one map; the campaign start ignores it.
- **Map 1's setup** in the campaign start gives you 6 starting squads, the Opinion Leader on the map, 3 random cards, 3 tech points, starting gold (see "Buildings and the economy") and a starting kit of items and supplies — see "Your starting army" below.
- **Building counts follow the map's land:** the default map gets 16 towns, 10 towers, 4 temples and 2 nomad camps (times the density setting); a map with more land gets proportionally more, one with less gets fewer. Hidden sites and lairs scale the same way. An islands map has less land, so fewer buildings than a continent of the same size. However small the map, it has at least 2 towns and at least 2 shops, one of them in a town (unless building density is set to none).
- The game opens on the battlemap with your one deployed squad standing on the Player Base. In single map mode Edit Units opens over it straight away, so you can look over (or build) your squads first (not in Quick start, whose squads are ready).

## The map

- **Hex grid**, axial coordinates, rendered in chunks for performance on large maps.
- **Terrain and difficulty for a squad not native to it:** Plains 1, Forest/Desert/Snow 2, Mountain/Swamp 3, Shallow water 3, Deep water/Lava/Cliffs/Air gaps impassable to ground squads. A squad's own travel-type terrain always counts as difficulty 1 for it, whatever the listed difficulty is. Every landmass is guaranteed reachable on foot from the rest of the map (a shallow ford, a mountain pass, a bridge, or similar), even if slow.
- **Map presets** (Continent, Islands, Archipelago, Floating Isles, Highlands, River Valley, Deep Forest, Tundra, Volcanic, No Ocean) change the terrain mix and favor different squad types; Continent is the default and most balanced. No Ocean is all land. Land % goes up to 100, except that Islands and Floating Isles stop at 70 and Archipelago at 60 so they keep their sea.
- **Getting to islands on foot:** every island is joined to the mainland by shallows you can wade, shaped like reefs or sandbars, wider than one hex and winding; a sizable island gets up to two more such crossings to its nearest neighbours. Roads never cross open sea, so an island's buildings have their own road network.
- **Buildings:** Player Base and Enemy Base (spawn points), towns and towers (liberate by standing a living ground unit in one for 60 ticks — twice as fast with a Sapper-line unit), temples (pay to heal/revive; free slow healing for anyone; taken like a town, but with no liberation rewards, and the enemy goes after them too, as a low priority), nomad camps (a town that relocates every half day and always has a shop), and hidden sites (see below).
- **Roads** connect the visible buildings and bases; a non-flying squad moves at ×0.75 time on them.
- **Day and night:** day 06:00–18:00, night 18:00–06:00. Holy units fight +15% by day and −15% at night; evil units the reverse; neutral units are unaffected.
- **Game speed:** five tiers (×0.25, ×0.5, ×1, ×2, ×4), PgUp/PgDn to change, Space to pause. A day at normal speed takes 5 real minutes. Opening any menu or a battle pauses the clock too. A new map starts paused, with "Press Play to begin" in the message banner until you first start time.
- **How many squads:** the configurator's "Squads on the map at once" is **Auto** by default: both sides field a force that grows with the map, about one squad per 6 towns, towers and temples (at least 4, at most 20), and the enemy fields as many squads as you may, besides the two that hold its base. You can still set a number by hand. **The cap grows as you take ground:** one more squad on the map for every 6 towns, towers and temples you hold now. Losing buildings lowers it again: squads already out stay out, but while you're over the cap none can deploy (one that comes home can't go back out). A banner says when it changes, and Edit Units and the deploy window show it ("On the map 3 / 5"); Edit Units' top bar also shows how many more buildings until the next one (a squad icon and "+1 in 2"; hover for the details). The enemy's grows the same way, one more squad for every 9 buildings it holds.
- **Skip to action:** while every squad of yours on the map is on the move, S (or the clock-with-speed-lines button just right of the speed menu, under the minimap) runs time fast until something needs you: a squad of yours arrives or stops, a fight or a neutral band, a building taken or lost (or any other news for the banner), an enemy coming into sight, a hidden site found, or noon. Then the game pauses and the banner says what (a fight or meeting opens its own window instead). S again stops it. Greyed out, the button says why (which squad is standing still).
- **The screen:** gold (a coin, the amount and the day's income and upkeep) and the reputation meter (a bar between a sad and a happy face; hover for the number) sit at the top right. Your squads on the map are in a drawer at the middle left: shut, each row shows the squad's leader, number and status; the arrow slides it open to show each squad's travel type, speed and alignment and its name too; click a row to find that squad, or double-click it for the squad menu. **Travel type, speed and alignment** (a small hex of the terrain the squad travels best on, its speed tier, and a sun, half circle or horned head; hover for details) show wherever a squad is shown: the squad menu's header, the drawer, and Edit Units. **Status icons** (in the drawer and the squad menu's header; hover for details): footprints, moving; a house, standing in any building; crossed-out footprints, standing in the open. **Squads on the map** are their leader standing on the map, in full colour (people as whole figures, monsters as heads), on a soft shadow edged in the side's colour (blue yours, red the enemy's; wider under a Large leader), with the number on a small tag at its feet; flyers hover above their shadow. A squad that lost its leader and is crawling home shows a skull in a ring instead (on the map and in the drawer). Zoomed out, a squad's click area grows with its token, a little wider still, so squads are easy to grab; where two then overlap, a small menu asks which you meant. **Towns with a shop** have a white dollar sign on the roof. **Flags mean destinations:** click a moving squad and its numbered flag stands where it's headed (the squad menu's **View destination** centres the map there and shows the flag for a few seconds); while you lay a move, the flag is your pointer. **Place names in banners are links:** click one ("The enemy has captured Ashford.") and the map centres on it. **The notifications log:** the open book just left of the gold (or N) opens a ledger of every notice the banner has shown (news, the noon report, battle results, portal trips), oldest first, starting with "Begin game." the moment you first start time, each with its day and time; it scrolls to the latest. Place names there are links too: clicking one closes the ledger and centres the map on the place. The ledger is saved with the game. Prompts (how to lay a move, Press Play) and quick replies to a click ("can't reach that hex") aren't recorded. The minimap is at the bottom right with the date, time and game speed under it (the time of day as an icon after the time: a sun by day, a moon at night, a sunrise in the hour before 06:00 and a sunset in the hour after 18:00; hover it for the word), and the toolbar (Edit Units, Deploy Squad, Cards, Tech) is at the bottom centre. The round **info button** at the bottom left shows or hides the map details and keyboard controls (hidden to start).
- **Fog of war:** terrain is always visible; enemy and neutral squads only show within your nearest squad's sight radius (base 7 hexes, further out while flying or on your own travel terrain, closer in mountains or forest, clamped 5–10 and never shown as a number). **Buildings you hold see too:** a tower 16 hexes around it, a temple 12, a town or the Player Base 8. Hidden sites reveal the same way. Enemy and neutral routes are never drawn on the map, only your own.
- **The log (L)** records every battle action, every map order and arrival, every economic flow, and every progression change, filterable by squad and category. A debug toggle in it shows the hidden numbers behind each entry (hit chance, the actual roll, growth, luck, and so on).

## Squads and movement

- Each squad has a **front row** and a **back row**; a unit's class decides whether it fights (and is attacked) from the front or back. Edit Units puts a unit in the back row when it heals or supports from there, or when its ranged or magic attack there reaches at least as many units as its front attack; otherwise in front. Units reached = hits × units per hit: a plain attack reaches 1 a hit, a row-wide attack 3, a chain or cleave its number (chain 3 = 3), a pierce 2, a hit on everyone 5. So a Sapper (3 melee hits in front, 1 shot behind: 3 against 1) goes in front, and a Siege Engineer (3 melee hits, 1 row-wide shot: 3 against 3, a tie goes back) goes in back. You can still put anyone in either row; this only picks the default and the attack its card shows. Any unit can move between the rows of its squad, anywhere, the leader included: it stays the leader, and the squad keeps the slots its leader earned. Front-row units take 20% less damage from every attack, since they take the brunt.
- **Every squad the game generates starts with its rows evened out**: yours, the enemy's and the neutrals'. 5 units split 3:2 or 2:3, 4 units 2:2, never 4:1. The units moved are the ones that fight about as well from either row. You can still rearrange your own however you like in Edit Units, even while a squad is out in the field. Adding units to a squad or taking them out only works at the Player Base.
- **Slots:** every squad starts with 5 (6 in single map mode). A Small unit costs 1 slot, Large costs 2, and two Tiny units can share a single slot. A leader earns +1 slot at level 5/10/15/20, but only if their charisma is at least 5 × their level at that moment (a Herald or Standard-Bearer in the squad helps toward this).
- **Travel type:** a squad's one native terrain, decided by a weighted vote of its living units' home terrain (Small 1, the leader and Large units 2, Tiny ½; ties go to the leader). Moving through that terrain costs the squad its base speed; other terrain costs more (see difficulty above). A squad that's entirely flyers moves as Sky (flies over everything but air gaps); a squad that's entirely water natives or amphibians can cross deep water.
- **Speed tier** is set by the squad's slowest living unit and decides how fast it crosses a hex; losing units to death slows the squad further, proportional to the slots lost.
- **A squad always has a leader** (in both modes): in Edit Units, only a unit that can lead can start a squad in an empty cell, and the last unit that can lead can't leave a squad that still has others in it (move the others out first, or bring in another leader). A message says why when a drop is refused.
- **Edit Units:** opening a squad (from the map's squad menu or a squad card in Edit Units) always shows the same Edit Squad view. Its title reads "Edit Units > Edit Squad: Kenley (Sapper)"; click "Edit Units" for the whole army. The X at the top right (or Esc, or a click outside) goes back to wherever you opened it from: the army view, or the map. The army view closes with Done (U). Each squad's card shows where it is at the top right and Deploy (or Return) at the bottom right. A unit's details are a character sheet (a ? beside its X opens the glossary) with two tabs: Character (the unit's icon in a gold frame, HP and XP, attributes, its unit details including whether it can lead, how it fights and promotes, and its equipment) and About (the same framed icon in the same place, with its class's description and promotion lines). In a unit's details, the arrows at the screen's edges (or the Left and Right keys) step through its squad: front row left to right, then the back row.
- **The Recruits list** in Edit Units: a gray star marks every recruit who can lead (a gold star is a squad's actual leader). A Sort by menu orders the list by Leader (the default: leaders first, then by stage and level), Class, Level or Name; the choice is kept until you quit. Each recruit is colored by the attack it would use in the row it would be placed in (a healer shows gold, an archer green), with a color key above the list: Melee, Ranged, Magic, Heal / buff, Debuff, Ward, Breath.
- **Deployment:** at the start, only the Opinion Leader's squad is on the map. Other squads deploy from the Player Base (via Edit Units or the Deploy Squad button, which is greyed out when the squad cap is reached or no squad is waiting at base), costing one day of their upkeep in gold, and appear on the Player Base; you lay their route as you deploy them. A squad cap (Auto by default, set in the configurator; see "How many squads") limits how many of your squads can be on the map at once. A squad returns to base by marching there and going in on arrival, or instantly if it's already standing on the Player Base.
- **Enemy squads:** clicking one you haven't fought only says how strong it looks. Once you've fought it, clicking it shows its units (class, level, row, and who had fallen) as they were when that fight ended.
- **Arrivals:** when one of your squads stops at the end of its route, a banner and the log say where ("… has stopped at Ashford").
- **Giving orders:** click a squad to open its menu (this pauses the game); Move lets you click hexes to lay a chain of rally points, and clicking the last point again commits the route. You can plan routes for as many squads as you like, one at a time, while paused.
- **Following:** while laying a move, click a visible enemy or neutral squad that is on the move and choose "Follow" ("Move here" just lays a rally point there; Esc closes the choice). A squad standing still can't be followed: clicking it just lays a rally point there. Clicking that rally point, or the squad standing on it, a second time commits the move without asking again. Your squad heads for it and re-routes whenever it moves, and catching it starts a fight as usual. If it slips out of sight, your squad goes to where it was last seen and stops there. Any new order ends following, and the squad menu shows "Following …" meanwhile.
- **The squad menu** shows the squad, front row above back, each unit as the enemy squad window shows one (icon, first name, class with a ? for the glossary, level, an HP bar, its attack from its row; moving units is for Edit Squad), and one row of icon buttons: Edit squad (pencil), Heal (a red cross: Herbs and Revival Draughts anywhere, and standing in a temple or on the Player Base, healing and reviving for gold; greyed out, saying why, when it has nothing to do: everyone at full HP, or away from a temple and the base with no Herb or Revival Draught that helps anyone. The same goes for Heal before a fight, where only supplies count. The Heal window shows the squad as Edit Units does, a tile per unit with its HP bar, front row above back, with a button under each tile for what can be done for it (each tile keeps that room when its buttons go, so nothing jumps), and your Herbs and Revival Draughts left as icon × count at the top), Shop (a hand with coins; greyed out in a temple), Move (footprints), Portal (in a temple; see "Temples you hold"), View destination (flag, while moving), Ranged attack (crosshair) and Return to base (house). Hover a button for its name and what it does, or why it's greyed out. Backing out of anything you opened from it (Edit squad, Heal, the shop, laying a move, a ranged attack, the portal) brings you back to the squad menu; only closing the menu itself, or an order that's done (Return to base, View destination), goes back to the map.
- **Closing windows:** an X at the top right closes a window (Esc too). Clicking outside a window also closes it (the squad menu, building, deploy, Heal, shop, tech, log, Edit Units, Cards, options, debug panel, a liberation message, and the Esc menu, which resumes; a yes/no question counts it as no). Windows that need an answer don't: a card choice, recruiting, the encounter dialog and the battle.
- **Squads of yours can share a hex** freely. A hex held by a hostile squad blocks movement; your squad waits at its edge instead of stacking on it.

## Units

- **Stats:** STR, CON (HP = CON × 4), INT, SPD (turn order), AGI (hit and dodge), PDEF, MDEF, plus Alignment (0–100: evil ≤40, neutral 41–59, holy ≥60) and Charisma (0–100).
- **Rolling a new unit:** starts from its class's base stats, adds a random number of "flex" points weighted toward the class's key stats, rolls its alignment inside its class's lean, and gets a unique first and last name (no repeats within a run).
- **Hidden traits**, rolled once and never shown directly: a growth profile (each non-key stat has its own chance to grow at a level-up, guaranteed at least once every 3 levels), a talent tier (Ordinary/Gifted/Prodigy, each raising XP and growth chances), and luck (a little extra crit chance, dodge, and better draws). These only ever show up as a visible effect — a Prodigy's level-up gets a flourish, a lucky unit does well more often.
- **Morale**, per squad (0–100, starts at 50, never shown as a number): falls when the squad's outnumbered in a battle, its leader gets hit, a unit of it dies, it fights off its leader's home terrain, or at the wrong time of day for its alignment (holy by night, evil by day); rises on a win, a liberation, or resting in a temple. Applies to every squad, not just yours — an enemy or neutral one has to be driveable-off too. Below 30, the squad deals 10% less damage; below 25, your leader can try to drive a beaten enemy off the map for good instead of letting it recover, for a charisma-based chance. A log line marks every change.
- **Loyalty**, recruited neutrals only (§16; a unit you roll fresh for a Serf-line squad never has it): rises after a win or while reputation is high, falls sitting unused in the recruit pool. If it drops very low, the unit gets a day's warning (logged) before it might actually leave the army for good on the next day change — never mid-battle.
- **Bonds:** two units that fight 6 battles together in the same squad gain +5% damage and +5% dodge while both stay alive, announced once in the log.
- **XP and levels:** every unit of yours still alive when a battle ends earns XP, whether you won or lost (a quarter as much if you retreated), more against a stronger enemy and with a more charismatic leader. A unit below its squad's average level earns 25% more per level under (at most double), so newcomers catch up. A level costs the class's XP rate × a fixed amount. Key stats always go up a level; other stats follow the hidden growth roll.
- **Promotion** becomes available at level 3, 6 and 9 (into the class's next line stage), anywhere (at the Player Base or out in the field), only if the squad still fits its slots afterward. The choices are listed one under another in the unit's details, each with a ? at its right that opens that class in the glossary; where a line branches by alignment, the unit's current band decides which branches are open.
- **Leaders:** whichever living unit that can lead was added to the squad first. If the leader is removed, the best remaining leader-capable unit (alive, highest level, highest stage) takes over automatically.

## Battle

- **Trigger:** a player squad and a hostile squad land on the same or adjacent hexes, or you order a ranged attack from the squad menu, or **an enemy squad fires on yours**: an enemy squad with archers or casters in position fires on the nearest of your squads within range (2 hexes, 3 from a mountain or tower; not one right beside it, which is a close fight) whenever its reload is over, by the same rules as yours (only ranged units act on each side, no push-back, a 2-hour reload). Squads falling back to recover don't fire. You're warned as for any fight (the dialog reads "X's party is under fire"), and the battle's middle box says who fired. In the battle, "Ranged Battle" shows under the round number (white on a dark band), and every unit that doesn't fire (no ranged or magic attack from its row) is faded to half, on both sides; everything else sits exactly where it does in a close battle. The map pauses and the camera glides to the fight, zooming in until the view spans about half the map (if you're already closer, it just pans), centred on it even at the map's edge, then a "X's party has encountered the enemy" dialog appears below the two squads. Heal there lets you use Herbs and Revival Draughts on that squad before the fight (a revived unit fights); it's greyed out when everyone is at full HP, and once healing brings everyone to full HP the Heal window closes by itself (from the squad menu too, which comes back with Heal greyed) and the dialog comes back (with an "All better!" voice line once it's recorded). **View Squad and Map** puts the dialog aside and pauses, with that squad's menu open: you can shop, heal, change its formation (Edit squad), or give any of your squads orders, and the dialog comes back, with the odds as they now are, as soon as you resume time. The two squads in the fight can't leave it meanwhile (no Return to base or portal), and no one fires a ranged attack until it's fought. Begin Battle opens the battle view, which springs open from the fight's place on the map. The battle is played on the defending hex's terrain. The battle view shows both squads, each unit as a card with its icon, class, name, attack (or a row of icons for its statuses) and HP bar, and waits for you to press Fight (Space), unless you turn that off in Options. Each action takes about 1.2 seconds at ×1 (×2 and ×4 are faster). A ranged attack you ordered yourself (not one fired at you), or a fight with neutrals (the recruit dialog already announced it), skips the dialog, since you already know it's coming.
- **Turn order:** every living unit with attacks left acts once per round, fastest (SPD) first, until both sides are out of attacks or one side is wiped. A squad that resisted recruitment, or has First strike/Charge, can act first.
- **Targeting:** plain melee hits the front row first (the back row only once the front is empty); ranged and magic can hit any row; several classes have group attacks (hit a whole row, the whole squad, a cleave, a pierce, a chain) per their class sheet.
- **Your controls mid-battle:** switch tactic any time (Best/Strong/Weak/Leader — who your units focus; Best finishes a unit off whenever a hit is expected to kill one, the most dangerous first, since every unit removed is damage that stops coming back; otherwise it picks the target the hit takes the most HP off, counting no more than the HP a unit has left), retreat, or play any cards in your hand. **Each squad keeps the tactic you last picked for it** into its next battles (and through a save) until you change it; a new squad starts on Best. Cards opens your hand large in the middle of the screen and the battle waits while it's open; Play takes effect at once, before the next attack (even before the first one, while the battle waits for Fight, and before a round's first attack while its banner shows); an attack already under way finishes first. The X (or Esc) goes back.
- **The battle view:** both squads on a field of the battle's terrain, each squad's name on a small tag above it, and in the middle a "vs." box with the terrain, time of day and who attacked. Your squad is always on the left, whoever started the fight. One button at the field's bottom left reads Fight before the battle starts, then becomes a small round Pause or Play icon (Space works too). While the battle plays, only the field shows, in an even frame with the space below it see-through, and the latest action is written beside the button; pause it and the play-by-play log and the other controls (speed, tactic, retreat, cards, items, skip) show under the field. The log marks each round "Begin Round N". A speed you pick in a battle is kept for the next battles. **What you see of the enemy:** only its HP bars, with no HP numbers and no attack labels ("Melee ×2"), and the log says how hard each blow landed but not what's left. A living Scout or Hussar (the Scout line) in your squad shows the enemy's numbers and attacks in full. **A closer look:** click an enemy unit's card and the battle holds while a small window shows its class, level, an HP bar (no numbers), the attack it makes from the row it stands in, and a ? for its glossary entry; never its attribute numbers. On the map, an enemy squad you've fought can be clicked while it's in sight of your squads or buildings for the same look at each of its units as they are now; out of sight it can't be clicked at all.
- **The result:** when the battle ends, the field goes away and a summary takes its place. Its title bar says Won, Lost, Retreated or Draw, from your side. Under that are two boxes, your squad above and the enemy below, with the outcome between them ("defeats", "loses to", "retreats from" or "draws with"), each with its leader's icon and, beside it, the side's name (the enemy's as "Enemy (<leader's class>)") with how many of its units fell in this battle under it, out of how many fought ("Units lost: 2 of 6"; no names). The winner is still decided by the share of its own HP each side lost, which the screen no longer shows. Then only the XP gained (bold, centred) and a tile for each unit that levelled up (its icon, "<name> reaches level N", what it gained, and a leader's new slots), two to a row, the offer to drive a shaken enemy off, and Continue. After a fight with neutrals the map's message reads "<your squad> wins" or "loses", plus "The rats are beaten back and return to the plains" or "The rats are victorious and return to the plains", with the battle's terrain.
- **Damage:** physical is (STR + weapon) × modifiers − (PDEF + armor); magic is (INT + spell power) × modifiers − MDEF, then an element multiplier; healing scales with INT. Hit chance is 85% plus the attacker's AGI edge over the target's, minus a little per target luck, clamped 50–95% (never shown as the raw number — you see the shaped, fairer version: two dice averaged, a guaranteed hit after 3 misses in a row, and crit chance that climbs after every non-crit). Damage always varies ±10%.
- **Modifiers** that multiply together: home terrain (+15%, or −15% on difficulty-3 terrain away from home), day/night by alignment, element match-ups (strong/weak pairs), a few class-vs-class bonuses (archers vs flyers, mounted vs mounted, and so on), the defending building's bonus, and each class's own traits and auras from the sheet.
- **Status effects** (poison, burn, freeze, shock, petrify, sleep, charm, blind, silence, fear, weak, haste, protect, regen, cleanse) all clear at the end of the battle; a unit native to an element's terrain resists the matching one.
- **Retreat:** your chance is 50% plus 20% per speed tier you have on the enemy, clamped 10–90%. Success steps your squad back a hex and costs each unit a point of charisma; failure costs the squad its next action.
- **How the enemy fights:** each enemy squad has a tactic of its own, rolled once and kept: usually Best, sometimes the strongest, the weakest or your leader. The battle's middle box says which ("They focus: the leader"), so you can set yours against it. An enemy squad that's clearly losing (it has lost at least a quarter of its HP, and at least twice the share your side has) tries to retreat at the start of each round from the second, with the same chance and the same failure as yours; getting away steps it back a hex and counts as your win. The Warlord, the base guard and holdfasts never retreat.
- **Outcomes:** a wiped-out squad is carried back to its base needing full revival; a squad that loses its leader marches home on its own at half speed (reviving the leader in time cancels this), only ever to its own base (the Player Base, or the Enemy Base for the enemy), taking no building on the way, and going round the other side's squads that stand in the way only when that's no more than about a third longer (yours only round enemies you can see); otherwise the side that lost the larger HP share gets pushed back 1 hex (2 if it wasn't on its travel terrain). A squad keeps its current orders after a battle unless it was pushed back, retreated, wiped out, or lost its leader. If the beaten squad still stands on the winner's route, the winner goes round it and carries on (for 30 game minutes after a fight the two won't fight again just for standing side by side). **But a squad ordered back at the squad it just fought** (sent, after the fight, to the hex that squad stands on) fights it the moment they meet, with no wait; this goes for the enemy too. **The enemy takes four times as long to capture a building you hold** as a neutral one, a window for you to answer it. **After any battle, an enemy squad's capture count doesn't move for 30 game minutes:** an enemy sitting in a town it's taking keeps the progress it had, but adds nothing until then. A ranged volley doesn't pause it. Your squads don't wait: one standing on the town starts (or goes on) liberating it as soon as the battle is over. An enemy squad that beats one of yours stands still for 30 game minutes afterwards, so you can come back at it. **Losing heart:** a squad (yours too) that loses 3 battles within a game day (beaten, or retreated; ranged volleys don't count) fights without any defensive bonus until the oldest of those losses is a day old: its PDEF and MDEF are its own (no terrain, tech, aura, card or Last stand boost), its front row takes full damage, a building gives it no defense, and protective traits, auras and dodge bonuses do nothing. Penalties still apply. A banner says when a squad loses heart. **Beaten squads give out:** a squad's PDEF and MDEF drop 5% for each battle it has lost (beaten, or retreated; not ranged volleys) since it last recovered and 5% for each of its units now fallen, at most 60% in all; an enemy squad's steps are half again as big (7.5%). Your squads recover once every unit is back at full HP; an enemy squad once it has finished recovering on a town it holds or its base. This is on top of losing heart. **A wiped-out enemy squad leaves the board** with a banner saying so, and comes back at the Enemy Base at full strength after half the time it would take that squad to march there from where it fell (the banner says how long). The Warlord's doesn't (beating him wins), and a squad driven off stays gone. If a beaten enemy's morale is low enough, your leader can try to drive it off the map for good instead of letting it recover (see "Morale" above); on success your squad earns XP as if it had beaten them: each living unit gets the battle's XP again. The battle results offer it as a button when it's on ("Demand they flee", with your leader's charisma as the chance, halved if their morale is 10–25; its tooltip says what it does). It's offered only at a 25% chance or better. Driving off the Warlord wins the game just as wiping out his squad does.

## Buildings and the economy

- **Towns have names** (Ashford, Old Rookmere), the same for the same map seed. They show in the building's popup, the liberation message and the log. **Towers have magical names** ("The Tower of Ee", "Amaraz's Tower of Surprises", "The Crooked Spire"), no two alike on a map. **Every temple belongs to one of the twelve Gods of Perinea** and is named for it ("the Temple of Ithrael"; two temples on a map can share a god). Its window shows the god's domain, what they look like and how they show themselves to mortals. Other buildings go by their type.
- **Hidden sites** stay off the map until one of your squads' sight reaches them; then they show what they are. Seeing one gives nothing: a squad has to step onto it.
  - **Treasure** (a chest): 100–250 gold, and half the time an item that needs no tech.
  - **Card cache** (a card): a card (a Seer or Oracle leader lets you choose, as usual).
  - **Rare recruit** (a tent with a banner): a stage 1 unit from a race line (never a sea creature), at the median level of your squads, would join you: a window shows who it is, with Recruit (it joins the recruit pool) or Leave (it stays there, the same unit, for later; step off and back on to be asked again). It counts as a recruit for loyalty.
  - These three are used up: the site leaves the map once claimed.
  - **Secret temple** (a small mossy temple): a temple in every way once found (free slow healing, and paid healing under Heal). It never shows on the minimap.
  - **Lair** (a cave): a neutral band native to the ground there guards it and never leaves it, as strong as a roaming band at that distance would be. Meeting it offers Recruit or Fight as with any neutral band, and after that it's gone either way. From then on any squad standing there defends +10% and heals 10% a day.
  - A town with local news points you at the nearest unseen site of any kind.
- **Clicking a building** anywhere on its picture opens its window. A building you don't hold that none of your squads can see shows only its name, who holds it (no one or the enemy) and whether it has a shop; the rest (defense, liberation progress, local news) shows once a squad is in sight or you hold it.
- **One thing at a time:** what the world brings (a fight, a neutral band, a rare recruit, a liberation, a building lost, a card offer, the noon ledger) opens only on the bare map, about a second after the last window closed, and not while a player tip is up; so two that happen together come one after the other. Tips about the map itself wait behind them too.
- **One liberation at a time:** when several come together, each waits until the one before has finished (its window closed, or its banner shown, and its riff and voice line played out), then a second's pause, so each one's sound plays with its own window and camera move.
- **Liberation message:** names the place and shows any card drawn on the left, a little larger than cards elsewhere; a hand-of-cards button with your card count at the bottom right shows your whole hand as cards, over the message; any conscripts who joined (single map mode) are listed on the right with their icon, name, class and level. The X closes it. When it closes, a small fireworks show bursts over the place, so you can tell which it was in a cluster. "Do not show this window again" (or Options) turns the message off: a banner names the place instead and the fireworks still play. It always opens when there's a card to choose. **Retaking a town or tower** you liberated before (it draws no card) opens a smaller window titled "<place> recaptured!" over the place's picture: "The people grudgingly accept your rule a second time. Try not to lose their trust again." (third, fourth... counted per building). With the window turned off, the banner says "recaptured".
- **Enemy captures:** a banner says when the enemy captures a neutral town or tower ("The enemy has captured Ashford."). When it retakes one of yours, a window says so instead ("Ashford is lost"), the map centres on the place, and it costs 3 reputation.
- **Every building starts neutral** except the two bases: no one holds a town or tower at the start, so both sides race out to take them. You can shop in, rest in and use any building the enemy doesn't hold; you only earn income from the ones you hold.
- **Liberating** a town or tower, neutral or enemy-held (stand a living ground unit there for 60 ticks, twice as fast with a Sapper) flips its owner, gives reputation and alignment swings, +5 charisma to a matching-alignment leader, a gold bonus, a card, and a tech point. A popup names what you took, focuses the camera on it, and shows the card you drew (or lets you pick one, if a Seer or Oracle offers a choice) right there.
- **Temples you hold:** taking a temple gives no bonus gold, reputation or card, and losing one costs no reputation (a "lost" window still shows). Taking one opens a window like a liberation's ("Temple of Hasketh taken!"), with its god and what holding it gives; the liberation option turns it off the same way. A held temple sees 12 hexes around it. **Portal:** a squad standing still in a temple you hold gets a Portal button (an orbit) in its squad menu. Pressing it returns you to the map, which pulls back to show the squad and every other temple you hold; those temples glow with pulsing rings. Click one and a question asks you to confirm ("Step through" or Cancel, which goes back to picking); then the squad is there at once, with the map following it. Esc goes back to the squad menu. In a temple you don't hold the button is greyed out and says why. **Training:** at each day's start, a squad standing in a temple you hold earns XP for each living unit: 30 ÷ its level (at least 1, times the XP rate), so a level 1 unit gets 30 and a level 6 one 5.
- **Resting in a building** heals a squad's living units a share of their max HP each day: 15% in your own base, 15% in a town/tower matching your alignment, 10% otherwise, 5% in a temple. Squads and the recruit pool at the Player Base heal the base's 15% too.
- **Income and upkeep**, settled every noon from day 2 (day 1 runs without one): you earn 50 gold per town/tower you hold, then each squad on the map pays upkeep for its units (fallen ones included). The recruit pool and squads at base cost nothing. A unit's upkeep goes by its stage and size (5/10/20/35 by stage, Large ×2), times the map's towns and towers ÷ 28 (a Small map's count), so a bigger map costs more and a tiny one less, in step with what it can pay. The HUD shows the day's income and upkeep next to your gold.
- **Starting gold** is 2 days' upkeep of a full field army on that map (the squad cap of 6-unit stage 2 squads), at least 500: 720 on a Small map.
- **Debt:** upkeep is paid even when the gold runs out, so gold can go below zero at noon. While in debt nothing can be bought (shops, Serfs, temple services, deploying); the gold shows red with "In debt 1/3", and a banner and the noon ledger say so. **Three noons running in debt, or debt past 2 days' upkeep of a full field army** (720 on a Small map), is a defeat. Getting back above zero resets the count. At noon the game pauses and shows the day's ledger (never taller than three quarters of the screen; a long day scrolls): every gold change since the last noon (liberation bonuses, purchases, deploys, temple services, sales, income, each squad's upkeep), the net for the day and the gold now. "Do not show this window again" (or Options) turns it off; a one-line summary shows in the banner instead.
- **Items** belong to the whole army: from Edit Units, any free item can go on any unit that can use it, and be taken off or swapped, wherever that unit's squad is, at base or out in the field.
- **Going broke:** at zero gold or in debt, any squad can be sold from Edit Units for a gold value based on its units' stages; it leaves the army, the map and loses its items.
- **Shops** open from the squad menu when a squad stands still in a building with one (not enemy-held). Towns and nomad camps also sell Serfs into your recruit pool. Every shop stocks Herbs, Revival Draughts, 4 of the 8 cure potions (which 4 is fixed per shop) and 1–6 kinds of random item, 1–4 of each (fixed per shop), priced by your reputation (and cheaper still for a squad with a Smuggler or Fence). Stock is limited: each line shows how many are left, and every shop refills to full at noon (except the map's one very rare item, which is gone once bought). A temple's paid healing and reviving is in the squad menu's Heal window, not a shop. **The shop screen:** the shopkeeper (a name and a greeting, the same each visit) at the top, then **Buy** and **Sell** tabs. Goods are listed on the left with how many are left and the price; pick one and the right side says what it does, who in the squad can use it and how it compares with what they wear in that slot, with a quantity picker, your gold before and after, and the Buy or Sell button. **Selling:** any item nobody wears (take it off in Edit Units first) and any supply sells for half its base price; reputation and Smugglers don't change what you get. **One shop on each map holds a very rare item** with a big buff (Sunblade, Stormbow, Dragonscale or the Crown of Ages, 400–450 gold); once bought it isn't restocked.
- **Services and consumables:** heal a hurt unit to full, or revive a fallen one, for gold — only at the Player Base, in the recruit pool, or for a squad standing still in a temple. Carried consumables:
  - **Herb** (+30 HP) and **Revival Draught** (a fallen unit back at full HP; reviving a leader cancels its march home): use them anywhere on the map from the squad menu's Heal.
  - **Cure potions**, one per status: Antidote (poison), Burn Salve, Thaw Tonic (freeze), Insulating Balm (shock), Softening Oil (petrify), Smelling Salts (sleep), Eye Drops (blind) and Echo Herb (silence). Use them in battle from the Items button, which lists only potions that fit a status one of your units has. A potion takes effect at once, like a card, and doesn't cost a turn. Statuses all clear after a battle anyway.

## Reputation, charisma and alignment

- **Reputation** is one army-wide 0–100 meter (starting at 50). It rises or falls with liberations and with time spent in a matching-alignment building, falls by 3 when the enemy retakes a building of yours, moving opposite ways for holy vs. evil squads (neutral squads don't move it). It sets your liberation bonus size, shop prices (cheaper near 100, pricier near 0) and your chance to recruit a neutral squad you meet — doubled for a squad with a Smuggler or Fence on prices.
- **Charisma**, per unit: up from liberating a matching building (leader only), from beating a notably stronger enemy; down from retreating or beating a much weaker one.
- **Alignment**, per unit, each battle: up against an even-or-stronger enemy, down against a weaker one; liberating a town pushes every unit's alignment toward holy, a tower toward evil.

Cards always show as their pictures: in the Cards window, the battle's card picker, a liberation's draw or choice, and when one is played in battle (the card itself appears mid-field for a moment). No limit on cards held or played. Every card is equally likely except Phoenix, which is rare (about 1 draw in 100), and Gazelle, half as likely as the rest.

The Cards panel (C) looks like the battle picker: your hand large in the middle of the screen, with Play under Rooster and Fox.

## Tech tree

- One army-wide tree, four tiers, six branches (Arms, Archery, Arcana, Cavalry, Beasts and flyers, Logistics) plus a terrain node per terrain per tier. You start with 3 tech points, earning more from liberations, battle wins, and a bonus for finishing the map in victory.
- A node costs its tier number in points. A tier opens once 80% of the previous tier's branch nodes (not counting terrain nodes) are bought. Spend any time from the Tech screen.

## Your starting army (Map 1 setup)

**Single map mode:**
- **An army that grows with the map.** Enough for one squad more than the "Squads on the map" cap (the Opinion Leader's included), full, plus spares: stage-3 leaders (one per squad and 2 spare), stage-1 Large units (one for every two squads), stage-2 units to fill every squad's 6 slots and 6 spare, and one Serf per squad. On a map whose cap is 7 that's 10 leaders, 4 Large, 38 stage-2 units and 8 Serfs. All from the Serf lines except the Large units, which come from the race lines (Drakeling, Wolf, Bison and the like; never a sea creature) — weak for their 2 slots at first, strong once promoted.
- Between them the leaders cover all 5 Serf-line terrains (Plains, Forest, Swamp, Mountain, Desert) before any repeats. Every stage-2 unit is a Plains unit, so a squad only travels on its leader's terrain when the leader has help in the vote: the Large units include one native to each Forest or Mountain leader's terrain (Swamp and Desert have none). The stage-2 units include at least one front-line unit and one healer or support unit per squad.
- **Auto pick army?** (the configurator's Army group, on by default) sorts all this into squads for you, as a starting point to change as you like: the Opinion Leader's squad 1 on the map, the rest waiting at the Player Base to deploy. Leaders with a monster of their own terrain lead first, with that monster, so those squads travel on Forest or Mountain; then the strongest leaders. Each squad without a monster gets one while they last, then a healer or support unit and a front-line unit each, and the rest fill the emptiest squads, each unit taking the row its squad is short of and a class it lacks (no second healer while others are left). Rows go by role (attackers in front, healers, casters and archers behind), balanced. Spare leaders and units, and the Serfs, stay in the recruit pool. Off, the Opinion Leader starts alone in squad 1 and everyone else waits in the pool for you to build squads from.
- **Squads hold 6 slots** (instead of 5), growing from there as usual.
- **Leaders decide how many squads you field.** Every squad needs a unit that can lead (stage 3 or 4; see "A squad always has a leader" above). You start with 2 leaders to spare; more come as units promote to stage 3 or leaders join, up to the squads the army holds (16, or the cap + 1 if that's more).
- **The Opinion Leader can move to any squad** or wait in the pool. Only their death loses the map (when that defeat is on), not squad 1 breaking up.
- **Conscription:** the first time you liberate a town (or nomad camp; not a tower), 1–3 fighters (Serf-line stage 2, at the median level of your squads) and a Serf join the recruit pool at the Player Base. If no one in the pool can lead, one of the fighters is a leader. Each town pays you once, however often it changes hands. The liberation message lists who joined.
- **The enemy grows too, at half your rate:** the first time it takes a town, 1–2 fighters (no Serf) join a pool at the Enemy Base, at its squads' median level. Once that pool holds 5 with a leader among them, they form a new Warband that marches out. The enemy fields no more squads than its cap: the squads it started with, plus one for every 9 buildings it holds (wiped-out squads waiting to come back count).
- **Enemy squads are bigger:** each has 5–6 units (its usual 4 plus 1–2 stage 2 units) and 6 slots, to match your squads of 6.
- Cards, tech points, gold, items and supplies as below.

**Quick start** (the Start screen's Quick Start, or Quick start mode in the configurator): single map mode in every other way (XP rate, conscription, bigger enemy squads, 6-slot squads), but your army is ready:
- **A small map:** 100×45 hexes, a Continent with 50% land and no offshore islands, at least 3 towns, 2 towers and a temple, and one town 8–15 hexes from your base.
- **4 squads**, each a stage-3 leader plus four stage-2 units, all from the Serf lines. Each squad has at least 2 front-line units (the leader counts) and at least 1 healer. One squad has a Large race-line monster (stage 1) in place of one of its stage-2 units. The leaders cover 4 different terrains.
- Squad 1 is the Opinion Leader's, on the map at the Player Base; the other 3 wait at base, ready to deploy.
- **The recruit pool** holds 3 Serf-line stage-2 units and 6 Serfs.
- **The first fight is close:** the town nearest your base is held by the enemy's **Garrison**, one of its field squads, which stays there instead of roaming (it still goes home to the Enemy Base after losing half its units). That town always has a shop. The enemy holds no other towns at the start.
- **Raiders:** one more enemy squad (5–6 units, like the others) waits on open ground about midway between the bases. After 4 game hours it marches on the nearest town you hold, or straight for your Player Base if you hold none, and takes it if nobody stops it (taking your base loses the map). Beaten down, it falls back to recover like any enemy squad, then comes again.
- Cards, tech points, gold, items and supplies as below.

**Campaign start:**
- **6 squads**, each a stage-3 leader plus four stage-2 units, rolled only from the basic "Serf" promotion lines (never the rarer race lines).
- Between the 6 leaders, every terrain a Serf line can reach (Plains, Forest, Swamp, Mountain, Desert) is covered by at least one squad's leader. Each squad also always has at least one front-line unit and one healer-or-support unit; everything else about the roll is random.
- One of the 6, chosen at random, is the Opinion Leader's squad — the only one that starts deployed, standing on the Player Base. The other 5 wait in your Edit Units roster.
- You also start with 3 random cards, 3 tech points, 500 gold, 6 random items that need no tech (in the Equipment tab of Edit Units), 5 Revival Draughts and 20 Herbs.

## The enemy

The enemy fields its Warlord and Base guard plus as many squads as you may have on the map (see "How many squads"). Each has 4 units, 2 front and 2 back, and no healers (5–6 units in single map mode, which also adds conscripted Warbands; see "Your starting army"). Its field squads split three ways, so the fighting starts in several places at once, not in one lane:

- **Raiders** (a quarter): set off from the Enemy Base and go anywhere on the map, keenest on buildings you hold (worth twice as much to them as to the front).
- **Holdfasts** (about one in seven): start holding the towns and towers nearest the Enemy Base, one each, named "Holdfast of <place>", and stay there like the Base guard.
- **The front** (the rest): a third of them start on towns of their own across the enemy's half of the map, picked far apart; the others start at the Enemy Base. A front squad only goes for buildings within reach of ground the enemy holds (its towns, towers and base: about 40 hexes on a default-width map, more on wider ones), so it pushes out from its territory instead of leaping across the map.
- **Forward squads:** besides those, one more front squad starts in each of the two quarters of your half of the map (either side of the line between the bases), on a neutral town or tower there about midway out from your base, which it holds for the enemy. They act like the rest of the front, so the first fights come sooner, even on small maps.
- **Quick start** is the exception: no holdfasts, spread starts or forward squads, but its Garrison holds the town nearest your base, and its Raiders come at you from midway (see "Your starting army").
- **The Warlord and a Base guard** stay on the Enemy Base and never go looking for a fight. They only move if pushed back from losing a fight there, or to come back after one.
- **Field squads** (the front and the raiders) march out to take towns and towers. Standing on one the enemy doesn't hold, a field squad stays until it's taken. Then it weighs holding it against moving on to another neutral or player-held town or tower that no other enemy squad is heading for:
  - **It wants you out of its backfield:** when a front squad first sees one of your squads (within 7 hexes), it may turn to chase it instead, more likely the more of its own buildings it would leave behind: 25% for each town, tower or base of the enemy's that your squad is nearer to than it is (so 4 or more, always). It follows your squad until they fight or it loses sight of it, then goes back to its plans. It rolls once per sighting: once your squad has been out of its sight (or after a fight), the next sighting rolls again. Raiders and holdfasts never chase.
  - Towns are worth more than towers, buildings toward the middle of the map are worth more than ones near either base, and a building you hold is worth double.
  - Holding is worth more the more of your strength is close by. It's worth little when nothing threatens the building, and nothing if another enemy squad already holds it.
  - A target is worth less the farther away it is.
  - It takes the better choice about 80% of the time and decides at random the rest. A squad that holds thinks again a few hours later.

**Enemy units only come back where the enemy holds ground, and slowly.** Any enemy squad that has lost half or more of its units (or its leader) drops what it's doing and falls back to the nearest town the enemy holds with none of your squads on it, or to the Enemy Base if there's none (if a fight on the way pushes it back, or that town falls, it sets off again for the nearest one). On the way it **goes round your squads that are standing still**, keeping a hex clear of each where there's a way round, so you have to move to catch it; it takes no account of squads on the move. **Raiders sneak:** about 3 times in 10, a raider setting off for a new target routes round all your squads the same way, slipping behind your lines. The Enemy Base is never given up: even with one of your squads standing on it, a squad heading home keeps coming and fights yours there, a last stand. Standing still in an enemy-held town or on the Enemy Base, its fallen come back one at a time, leader first, at full HP, one every 2 game hours; it stays until all are back, then returns to its post (or its next target). Living units don't heal there at all. An enemy squad with lighter losses carries its fallen until it next stands still in one. So the Warlord can be worn down: hit him with several squads in quick succession before his fallen come back. The enemy can retake a building the same way you liberate one: a living ground unit of theirs sitting unopposed in it flips it back, including your own Player Base. A building you hold takes them four times as long as a neutral one (240 ticks against 60), time for you to answer.

## Neutrals and recruiting

- **How many:** the Neutrals Density setting decides how many lairs there are (about 11, 14 or 20 on the default map for low, medium and high), one roaming squad each.
- **Lairs** are scattered off-road, each home to one race (never a Serf-line class) native to that spot's terrain; the further a lair is from the Player Base, the stronger and higher-level its squad. Unlike towns and towers, you can't tell a lair is there until a squad's sight reaches it.
- Each lair's squad **roams** a short patrol around its lair, chasing down the nearest squad of yours that wanders into range; with nothing nearby it just wanders near home. **Neutrals ignore the enemy** by default (a configurator setting, in the Neutrals group; on in Quick start): bands only fight you, and enemy squads pass them by. Turn it off and they fight the enemy too (an enemy squad that stumbles into one fights it out on its own, with no pause for you to watch), but never while either stands in a base, so a band can't wipe out the Warlord at home and hand you the win.
- **Meeting one yourself** glides the camera to it, like a battle, and offers a choice: you're told its class, not how many of them there are, then pick Recruit or Fight. **A leader who knows the beasts:** if your squad's leader shares the band's home terrain, the window says so and shows the chance on Recruit and on Sneak past.
  - **Recruit** rolls a chance from the neutrals' numbers, how far your squad's alignment is from theirs, and your reputation; success adds them to your recruit pool for free and gives your leader +3 charisma, no fight at all; the window then reads "Recruitment successful!" and says they wait in the recruit pool at the Player Base, ready for Edit Units.
  - **Sneak past** tries to slip by without a fight. The chance is 40%, plus 10% for each point your squad's average AGI is above the band's (or minus 10% for each point below), at least 15%, at most 100%; the result says what it was. Unseen, you pass, and the two won't meet again for 2 game hours (a roaming band may find you after that). Spotted, the fight starts and the band strikes first, as after a refused recruit; beating them still offers the recruit chance. Not offered after you've beaten a band. Failure means they fight you having resisted — they get a full round of attacks before you do. Either way the same dialog then tells you what happened before anything else: who joined, or that they refused, with the chance and the main thing that held it down.
  - **Fight** skips the roll and goes straight into a normal battle.
- **A second chance:** if you win the battle without wiping them out, the dialog asks once more: Recruit (the same chance, worked out on who's left) or Let them go. They join at the HP they have left; if they refuse, or you let them go, they go home.
- **You meet each neutral squad once.** After you recruit it or fight it, win or lose, it leaves the map; it never comes back at you as a second attacking force.
- This is the only way the rarer race lines (the sheet's R, N, L and T lines) enter your army — they never appear in a starting squad or an ordinary promotion.

## Victory and defeat

- The configurator turns each condition on or off; any one you've enabled ends the map the moment it's met (checked every tick). If both a defeat and a victory condition are met on the same tick, defeat wins.
- **Taking the Enemy Base** (liberating it, as with any building) wins at once, whatever conditions you've set.
- **Victory**, any you've enabled: defeat the enemy leader (on by default — their squad never moves from the Enemy Base, so this always means beating or driving it off there); reach the goal (stand a living squad on the Enemy Base's hex); defeat a set number of enemy squads (every wipe-out or drive-off counts, even of a squad that later comes back), or all of them (none on the board and none waiting to come back); hold a share of the map's towns and towers for a set stretch of time running (losing the share partway through resets the clock); or just survive until a timer runs out.
- **Defeat**, any you've enabled: the Opinion Leader dies (on by default); the Player Base is captured — the enemy takes it the same way you liberate a town, so this only becomes a real threat once you're not holding ground for them to march on instead. Always on: **death by penury**, three noons running in debt or debt past the limit (see "Debt").
- The map freezes the instant it ends — no more movement, battles or ticking — and the end screen shows the result and why, and the map in numbers, counting up: days, battles won and lost, places liberated, enemy squads defeated, towns and towers held, squads in the field, units in the army, reputation and tech points.
- **A victory pays a reward:** a day's income, plus 25 gold for every town, tower and temple you hold, shown under the numbers with your gold at the end. A won map's screen also plays the victory video (once, then its closing loop) with fireworks. From there the only way out is back to the title; there's no second map yet.

## Saving and loading

- **Save game** in the Esc menu opens 3 slots, each showing its day and time, gold, squads on the map and when it was saved (or "Empty"). Saving over a slot in use asks first. There's no autosave.
- Not during a battle or an encounter (the button is greyed out, and says why), and not once the map is over.
- **Return to title** and **Quit** ask "Save your game before leaving?": Save first (pick a slot, then leave), Leave without saving, or Cancel.
- **The start screen** shows **Continue** (loads the most recent save) and **Load game** (pick a slot) once any slot is in use. A loaded game opens paused, exactly as it was saved, and plays on just as it would have.
- A save from an older version of the game, or a damaged file, is refused with a message rather than loaded.

## Options

While time is stopped on the map, a large pause sign and "Paused" show at the top left.

Options on the Start screen and in the in-game menu (Esc) hold your own settings, saved between sessions. **Sound** comes first: a master volume (80 to start), Music (70), Effects (80) and Voice (100) sliders, and Mute all sound; changes are heard at once. The switches below are all on by default: whether a battle shows both squads and waits for Fight (a checkbox next to Fight turns it off too), whether switching to another window pauses the game (the battle, if one is playing, otherwise the map; you resume it yourself), the noon ledger, the liberation message, and player tips. **Pause game after losing battle?** is off by default; on, the map stays paused after a battle you lose until you press Play. The game also remembers the battle speed you last picked, and which tips you've seen.

## Sound

Sound is coming in (ROADMAP M5). In so far:

- **A click** on every button.
- **Battles:** "Fight!" as a battle starts playing; on a win, the victory fanfare and "Victory!"; on a loss, just "Defeat!" (no trombone since 2026-11-14). A retreat or a draw has no sound.
- **Hidden sites:** "Hidden Location!" when one comes into view; a short glockenspiel-and-trumpet sound when you claim a treasure, card cache or rare recruit. When a site comes into view, gold rings spread from it for a few seconds, and if it's off screen the map glides there (keeping its zoom).
- **The map's ambience:** under the piano, a very quiet bed of wind and grass by day, crickets and low wind by night, crossfading at dusk and dawn; now and then (about once a minute or so) a sound: songbirds, a banner, a gust by day; an owl, a crow, creaking timber, a gust by night. Some only when they fit: hooves while a mounted unit is on the move in sight, waves when a squad of yours is near the sea, a river when one is beside a river. Each sound comes from a little to the left or right, for depth; the bed stays in the middle. Only on the map, not in the menus, Edit Units or battles. These are first drafts, to be replaced.
- **Battle sounds:** each attack in a battle sounds like what made it: a sword, a club, a lance, claws, a bite, a bow, a crossbow, a sling, a dragon's roar, or its element's magic (fire, ice, water, wind so far); healing and the other support skills share one shimmer for now. Some are still to come (earth, lightning, holy and dark magic, plain magic, a miss, a unit falling) and stay silent until then. They're kept quiet under the music, and a sound doesn't stack on itself.
- **Buildings changing hands:** taking a building plays its riff as its liberation window opens (a town a banjo, a temple a pan flute; a tower has no riff, just its voice line) (or with its banner, if the window is off), so liberations sound one at a time, each with its own window. The enemy taking a neutral building makes no sound. The enemy taking one of yours plays its own falling, ominous sound with its "lost" window. The fireworks over a liberated building pop with each of their 5 bursts. After the riff, a voice line: "New Town!" (or Tower, Temple) the first time a building is ever yours. Retaking one the enemy took from you has no voice line.
- **Taking turns:** fanfares, riffs and voice lines never play over each other: each waits for the last to finish, and one that would wait more than 2 seconds is skipped. The same sound never plays over itself. Clicks and fireworks play at once.
- **Recruiting:** a recruit attempt that succeeds, or a rare recruit joining, will have a short welcoming sound (a harp and two horn notes).
- **Music:** the title screen plays a few soft piano phrases with short pauses between; the other menus play the map's day music, and it carries on into the map. On the map the music is sparse piano phrases with silence between them, never the same phrase twice running: a brighter set by day, a darker, lower one by night. At 18:00 the phrase playing finishes and a short dusk turn leads into the night set; at 06:00 a dawn turn leads back. It keeps playing while the game is paused. Edit Units has a march; a battle has a fast battle theme whose instruments change with the battle hexs terrain (plains, forest, mountain and snow, swamp and desert); a won map's end screen has broader, fuller piano phrases in the same way, a lost one a defeat tune. Each change crossfades over 2 seconds, and after Edit Units or a battle the map music starts a new phrase. Music dips while a fanfare, riff or voice line plays. (These are first drafts; the tunes may change.)

- The first time you reach certain parts of the game, a small tip appears beside the part of the screen it's about, with an arrow and an outline pointing at it. You can carry on using the screen while it's up. **Got it** (or Esc, or the X) dismisses it for good. Untick **Show player tips** on any tip, or in Options, to turn them all off.
- **So far:**
  - The second time Edit Units opens in a single map game (or a Quick start one), a tip points at the Recruits list: "To begin creating a new squad, drag a leader into an empty slot. To add to a squad, drag any unit into an active squad slot."
  - When you first reach the map (after that opening Edit Units), a tip points at Deploy Squad: "Use the deploy button to send your squads out into the field of battle after composing them in the edit units screen. Conquering buildings will provide bonuses and abilities."
  - **Quick start** opens with the map pulling back to show your base and the town the Garrison holds, and a tip points at that town: it names it and says how to send your squad there (click the squad, Move, click the town twice) and to press Space. The tip follows the town if you move the map.
  - The first time Edit Units opens (any game mode), a tip in the middle of the screen: this is where you arrange squads before deploying them; your first squads are assembled for you, change them as you like; Done (top right) returns to the map. The next time it opens, the tip on building a squad (in single map and Quick start).
  - Straight after it, if losing the Opinion Leader loses the map, a tip points at their squad's card: "This is your Opinion Leader's squad. If your Opinion Leader, <name>, falls, you lose this engagement."
  - Once time is running, the first time the Player Base is on screen (if losing it loses the map), a tip points at it: "This is your home base. Defend it with your strongest soldiers, for if it falls, you lose this engagement."
  - The first time you meet a neutral band (its Recruit, Fight or Sneak past window), a tip beside it: you'll meet unallied bands who may join your cause or prove violent; engage or avoid them as you choose; they vary greatly in power.
  - The first time the encounter dialog opens before a fight (any game mode), a tip beside it explains Heal, View Squad and Map (paused: shop, heal, change the formation or move other squads; the dialog returns when you resume), and that Begin Battle opens the battle, where you pick a tactic and press Fight.
  - The first time a town's liberation window opens, a tip beside it explains reputation from liberating towns, town income at the end of the day, and the shop sign on a town's roof; the first tower's does the same for towers (reputation from negatively aligned characters, income for your armies).
  - The first time you start time in a game (and only once ever), a tip points at the speed menu: it sets the speed and pauses; PgUp and PgDn change the speed and Space pauses and resumes; the skip to action button beside it runs time until one of your squads is interrupted. It waits for any tip already showing to be closed first.
  - The first time a squad of yours stops somewhere that isn't a building to liberate, a tip points at the squad list at the left: it's easy to lose track of which squads are moving, which are standing and where; the list finds each one, a double-click opens its menu, each has an icon for moving or standing, and the control at the top opens and closes it.
  - The first time you hold two temples at once, a tip in the middle of the screen: "Temples are not only places of worship; they contain portals that connect the land. When you control a temple, you can teleport instantly to any other temple you hold."
  - The first time a squad's menu opens, a tip points at its row of buttons: what each order is, and that hovering a button says what it does or why it's greyed out.
  - The first time a battle's results show, a tip beside them explains healing: Heal (the red cross) in the squad menu uses Herbs and Revival Draughts anywhere, and a temple heals and revives for gold.
  - The first time a battle against a squad with a Spearman starts, the battle pauses with its controls open and a tip points at the Tactic menu: some squads need different tactics; try Leader (focus the enemy leader).
  - The first time Edit Units opens while your army has a flying unit, a tip says flyers can't take buildings: a squad needs a unit on foot.
  - **Reset player tips** (on the Options screen, under Show player tips) asks first, then makes every tip show again the next time its moment comes. It's greyed out while no tip has been seen.
  - A tip counts as seen once it's gone, even if you close the window it was beside first. Tips wait their turn: one that comes due while another is up shows once that one is closed (a window's tip doesn't wait behind one left on the map under it). If its window closes first, it isn't counted as seen and comes again next time.

## Testing tools (debug panel)

Backtick opens the debug panel. It's for testers only and isn't part of the game. Each tab groups its tools in labelled sections; close it with the X, backtick or Esc. "At the cursor" means the hex under the mouse when you opened the panel.

- **Map:** reveal every enemy and hidden site; a fresh map from any seed with the same settings; map overlays (terrain, movement cost, roads, buildings per quadrant, lair danger), the hex grid, and your squads' sight radius.
- **Time:** jump to any hour, run the map on for N hours, or freeze the time of day while everything else keeps going.
- **Units:** *Build a squad*: an empty squad is shown in front and back rows; pick a class and a level and Add puts a freshly rolled unit of it into the squad (any mix of classes, up to a full squad, each in its usual row; the first that can lead leads). Click a unit to select it, then Remove. Pick a side and Place at cursor puts a copy on the map; the built squad stays in the panel, even after closing, to place again, until Clear. *A squad on the map*: pick one, then set its alignment, teleport it to the cursor or remove it. *Your army*: heal and revive all your units; the Wide roster button (16 varied squads).
- **Resources:** add gold, tech points or any card.
- **Battle:** force every battle to a win, loss or draw; replay the last battle with the same rolls (on copies, so nothing on the map changes; cards played and tactic changes made the first time aren't replayed); show hidden numbers in the log and battle view; switch off each fairness rule.
- **AI:** show every enemy and neutral squad's role and whether it's moving, with a line to its target; freeze enemy and neutral AI.
- **Simulation:** fight two squads' builds against each other N times and report win rates and HP lost.

Outside the panel: O cycles overlays, G the grid, H hidden sites, F8 a squad's travel type.
